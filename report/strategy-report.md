# Executive strategy memo | £1m growth challenge

**Case:** Historical UK e-commerce retailer · **Status:** Analysis pending official dataset execution · **Date:** October 2026

## Executive question
Where could a retailer look for incremental revenue, and what would have to be true to generate an additional **£1m per year**?

## Initial decision framework (not empirical findings)

**1 — Protect high-value repeat customers.** Use purchase-frequency and RFM analysis to determine the share of observed revenue associated with returning customers. *Hypothesis:* targeted re-engagement might improve repeat ordering. **Test:** randomised retention outreach where possible, with incremental gross profit, not just gross revenue, as the success metric.

**2 — Find opportunities within the product portfolio.** Examine products contributing most to purchase revenue and whether concentration or seasonality creates operational risk. *Hypothesis:* better availability and merchandising may protect revenue. **Test:** compare stock-out-adjusted performance where operational records exist.

**3 — Investigate cancelled transactions.** Quantify cancelled/returned line counts and flag potential revenue leakage. *Hypothesis:* improvements to product information or fulfilment might reduce avoidable cancellations. **Test:** collect reasons and matching refund records before attributing causes.

## What can be measured from this dataset?
Observed purchase revenue, monthly purchase volume, average observed order value, product ranking and repeat-order share **among known customer IDs**.

## What cannot be measured from this dataset?
Website conversion, seller onboarding, ad effectiveness, CAC, margin, net revenue after refunds, or causal campaign lift. Transaction records alone cannot establish why the patterns occurred.

## The £1m target
The `R/growth_scenarios.R` script shows assumptions under which a £1m uplift *might* be feasible for an illustrative £10m baseline. The example is not calibrated to observed data. It assumes additive lever effects, which must be revised to avoid overlapping or double-counted revenue.
